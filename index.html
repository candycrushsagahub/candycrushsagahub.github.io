About the game

Candy Crush Saga is a match-3 puzzle game developed by King. Each level shows a board filled with brightly coloured candies. You swap two neighbouring candies to line up three or more of the same kind, which clears them from the board and scores points. New candies tumble down to fill the gaps, and with a little luck — or a little planning — one match sets off a chain of satisfying cascades.

The idea is simple, but the goals keep changing. Some levels ask you to clear jelly from underneath the candies, others want you to drop ingredients to the bottom of the board, collect a set number of specific candies, or reach a target score before your moves run out. Matching four or five candies creates special candies — striped, wrapped and the famous colour bomb — and combining two specials together produces the big board-clearing moments the game is known for.

More than a decade after launch, the game is still growing. King adds fresh episodes regularly, and the level count has passed ten thousand. It remains one of the most downloaded games on the planet: industry tracker AppMagic estimates it picked up roughly 111 million new downloads in 2025 — around 9 million fresh installs every month.

How to Play Candy Crush Saga

Candy Crush Saga takes about thirty seconds to learn and thousands of levels to master. This guide covers everything a new player needs: how matching works, what the level goals mean, how special candies and combos are made, what boosters do, and how the lives system keeps the game ticking.

The basics: swap and match

Every level presents a grid filled with candies in up to six colours and shapes — red jelly beans, orange ovals, yellow teardrops, green squares, blue rounds and purple clusters. Tap a candy and drag it up, down, left or right to swap it with its neighbour. If the swap creates a straight line of three or more candies of the same colour, they pop, you score points, and new candies fall from the top to replace them.

A swap that makes no match simply swaps back, so feel free to experiment. Most levels give you a fixed number of moves rather than a timer, which means you can take your time and think. Finishing the goal before the moves run out wins the level; your remaining moves convert into bonus points and the all-important stars — one, two or three depending on your score.

Know your level goal

Before the first move, look at the goal panel. There are a few types you will meet again and again:

  • Score levels — reach the target score within the move limit. Big combos and cascades are your friends here.
  • Jelly levels — some squares sit on jelly. Make a match on top of a jelly square to clear it; some squares carry a double layer that needs two matches.
  • Ingredients levels — cherries and nuts appear on the board and must be guided down to an exit row, usually marked with arrows.
  • Order levels — collect a set number of specific candies, special candies or combinations shown in the goal panel.
  • Mixed modes — later episodes combine two goals, such as clearing jelly and collecting orders, inside one move limit.

First habit to build

Always make your matches count toward the goal. Clearing three candies in a corner with no jelly, no blocker and no ingredient nearby is usually a wasted move — unless it sets up something bigger.

Special candies: matching four and five

Matching more than three candies creates a special candy that stays on the board until you activate it by matching it or swapping it with a neighbour:

  • Striped candy — match four in a row. When fired, it clears a whole row or column. The direction of the stripes shows which way the blast goes, and the stripes follow the direction of your final swap.
  • Wrapped candy — match five in an L or T shape. It explodes in a 3×3 burst, then explodes a second time before disappearing.
  • Colour bomb — match five in a straight line. Swap it with any regular candy and every candy of that colour on the board pops at once.
  • Jelly fish — match four in a 2×2 square. The fish swims off and lands on a jelly square (or another useful target) and clears it.

Combos: where the big clears live

Swap two special candies into each other and something much bigger happens:

  • Striped + striped — fires both a row and a column blast from one point, clearing a cross through the board.
  • Striped + wrapped — becomes a giant striped candy that wipes three full rows and three full columns.
  • Wrapped + wrapped — a double explosion far larger than a single wrapped candy, twice.
  • Colour bomb + striped — turns every candy of the striped candy's colour into a striped candy, then fires them all at once.
  • Colour bomb + wrapped — clears all candies of two colours in two huge waves.
  • Colour bomb + colour bomb — the rarest move in the game: it clears the entire board.

Learning to spot a combo one move before it exists — "if I move this candy down, those two specials will sit side by side" — is the single biggest step from beginner to confident player.

Boosters: a helping hand

Boosters are helper items. Some are selected before a level begins and change your starting board: a colour bomb already placed, striped and wrapped candies scattered ready, or jelly fish swimming in from the start. Others are used during play:

  • Lollipop Hammer — smashes any single square you tap: a candy, a jelly layer or one layer of a blocker.
  • Free Switch — swaps any two neighbouring candies without needing a match and without costing a move.
  • Extra moves (+5) — offered when you run out of moves close to the goal, in exchange for gold bars.

You earn boosters by finishing episodes, spinning the daily booster wheel, opening the daily calendar and playing events. They are precious — our Boosters Guide explains each one in full, and the Tips & Tricks page explains when they are actually worth spending.

Lives: how the energy system works

Starting a level costs one life, and you hold a maximum of five. Failing a level costs the life; winning refunds nothing but costs nothing more either — you simply spend one life per attempt, win or lose. A lost life refills automatically after a waiting period, so a full set returns if you take a break.

There are kinder ways to keep playing: friends who play the game can send you lives, events and the daily calendar sometimes grant unlimited lives for 15 minutes, 30 minutes or longer, and gold bars can refill your lives instantly if you choose to buy them. Connecting the game to Facebook or a King account also saves your progress, so your level, stars and boosters follow you to a new phone.

Your first session, step by step: download the game from the official store, play the opening levels to learn swapping, connect an account so progress is saved, and resist spending your early boosters — the levels that truly need them are still ahead of you.

Tips & Tricks for Hard Levels

Every Candy Crush Saga player eventually meets a level that eats all five lives and asks for more. Hard levels are not luck tests — they reward a different way of playing. These are the strategies experienced players rely on, written from the mechanics of the game itself.

1. Read the board before you touch it

Spend the first ten seconds studying, not swapping. Where is the jelly concentrated? Which blocker will spread if ignored — chocolate grows into empty neighbouring squares, so it outranks almost everything else. Where do ingredients need to exit? A level is usually lost in the first five moves, when random matches at the top shuffle nothing that matters while the real problem sits untouched at the bottom.

2. Play from the bottom

Matches low on the board make the candies above them fall the furthest, which creates the most new combinations and the most accidental cascades. A match at the very top changes almost nothing. When two moves look equal, choose the lower one. This single habit is worth several levels of progress on its own.

3. Build special candies on purpose

Random matching wins easy levels. Hard levels are designed around special candies: a striped candy aimed at a row of jelly, a wrapped candy parked next to a blocker cluster, a colour bomb saved for the colour that covers the most jelly. Before each move ask, "does this move create a special, move a special closer to another special, or fire one where it hurts the level most?" If the answer is no three moves in a row, you are probably drifting.

4. Prioritise the goal, not the score

On a jelly level, a spectacular cascade in a jelly-free corner scores points and achieves nothing. Keep your eyes on the goal counter. The same applies to orders: if the level wants twenty blue candies, a match of three blues in a dull corner beats a flashy match of reds in the middle.

5. Deal with blockers early

Blockers compound. Chocolate spreads each time you make a move that doesn't touch it. Licorice swirls soak up special candy blasts that you needed elsewhere. Frosting layers hide jelly underneath. Clearing one layer of a stubborn blocker every few moves keeps the board open; leaving blockers until the end usually leaves you two moves short.

6. Watch the edges and corners

Jelly in corners and along edges is the hardest to reach, because fewer matches can touch it and striped candies rarely point there. Clear edge jelly early while the board is still full of options, and aim jelly fish and colour bombs at the colour sitting on those squares.

7. Save boosters for genuinely stuck levels

Boosters feel plentiful early and scarce late. A good rule: never spend a booster on a level you have tried fewer than five times, and never spend two on the same attempt. The lollipop hammer is best saved for the very last jelly square or the final layer of a blocker when you are one move from winning — not for opening a level you haven't understood yet. Pre-level boosters like a starting colour bomb are worth most on levels whose goal matches them: colour bombs shine on order and jelly levels, less on ingredient levels where board position matters more.

8. Use the shuffle and quit tricks honestly

If a level's opening board offers no useful moves toward the goal, you can leave before making a move without losing a life — on most versions, exiting before your first swap keeps the life. Re-entering gives a fresh board layout. It will not make a bad strategy work, but starting with a board that already contains a match of four is a real head start.

9. Take the hint system with a pinch of salt

Wait a few seconds and the game will wiggle a suggested move. The suggestion is simply a legal match, not the best one — it happily points at matches that ignore the goal entirely. Use it to spot matches you missed, never as your strategy.

10. Step away instead of tilting

Hard levels are deliberately placed as pacing bumps between easier stretches, and the same level often falls on the first attempt of a fresh session — new board layouts, a calmer eye, and a full set of lives. If a level has beaten you ten times, close the game, let your lives refill, and come back. Frustration makes players match the first thing they see; patience is a genuine mechanic in this game.

The 30-second pre-level checklist

Goal type? Worst blocker? Where is the jelly or exit? Which colour covers the most goal squares? Answer those four questions before move one and hard levels become puzzles instead of lotteries.

Stuck right now?

Re-read the checklist, open the game, and try the level once more — bottom first, blockers early, specials on purpose.

Candy Crush Saga FAQ

Straight answers to the questions players ask most — cost, offline play, lives, boosters, syncing and download size.

Is Candy Crush Saga free to play?

Yes. Candy Crush Saga is free to download and free to play. Every level can be completed without spending money. The game does offer optional in-app purchases, such as gold bars and extra boosters, but they are never required to keep progressing.

Can I play Candy Crush Saga offline?

Yes, most levels can be played without an internet connection once the game is installed. You will need to go online occasionally to sync your progress, receive lives from friends, join events and download new episodes.

How do I get more lives?

Lives refill on their own over time, up to a maximum of five. You can also receive lives from friends who play the game, earn unlimited-lives rewards from events and the daily calendar, or refill instantly with gold bars if you choose to.

What are boosters?

Boosters are helper items that make a level easier. Some are chosen before a level starts, such as a starting Color Bomb or Striped and Wrapped candies, and others are used during play, like the Lollipop Hammer, which smashes any single candy or blocker on the board. You earn boosters from events, the daily booster wheel and level rewards. The Boosters Guide explains every one.

Does my progress sync with Facebook?

Yes. If you connect the game to Facebook or create a King account, your level progress, gold bars and boosters are saved to that account. Signing in with the same account on a new phone or tablet restores your progress.

Is Candy Crush Saga good for kids?

The game itself is a gentle, cartoon-style puzzle with no violence, and it is rated for everyone on the app stores. Parents should know it contains adverts and optional in-app purchases, so it is worth turning on purchase approval or disabling in-app purchases in the device settings for younger players.

How big is the download?

The iPhone and iPad version is roughly 320 MB on the App Store, while the Android size varies by device. The game also downloads extra level data as you play, so leave a little free space beyond the initial install.

Why is this level so hard?

Difficulty rises and falls on purpose: very tough levels are spaced between easier ones so the game stays interesting. Hard levels usually have a tight move limit, stubborn blockers like chocolate or licorice, or an objective that needs special candy combinations. Studying the board before your first move, working near the bottom, and saving boosters for these levels makes a real difference — see our Tips & Tricks.

How many levels are there?

Well over ten thousand, and King adds new episodes regularly, so the total keeps growing. Most players will never run out of levels to play.

Can I play on a computer?

Yes. As well as the Android and iOS apps, the game can be played on Windows through the Microsoft Store and in some regions through Facebook in a web browser. Progress carries over when you sign in with the same account.

Still deciding?

It is free, it works offline, and the first levels take minutes. Download it from the official stores and see for yourself.
